# Model T — a car for everyone

Research question: how did Ford make a car everyone could buy? In 1909 a car was a rich man's toy. By 1917 Ford sold 785,432 a year at $360. What did it?

## 1. One model, no options #product

> "Any customer can have a car painted any colour that he wants so long as it is black." —— Ford, 1922

👩 He cut the menu to one dish, and sales hated it. You can't build one line for eight models, so this decision comes first.

## 2. Price first, cost follows #business

> "Therefore we first reduce the price to a point where we believe more sales will result. Then we go ahead and try to make the price." —— Ford, 1922

👩 Most companies do cost + margin = price. He did it backwards: name the price, then force the cost down to meet it.

> [!claude]
> That is his stated policy, and his own table fits it: $950 → $360 while output went from 18,664 to 785,432 (1909–17). What the table can't show is which came first. Price and volume moved together; the "price led" part is Ford's account.

## 4. The $5 day was a PR stunt #team

👩 Doubling pay the same month the line made the work dull. Looks like advertising.
