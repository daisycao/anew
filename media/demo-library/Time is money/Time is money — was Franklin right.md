# Time is money — was Franklin right

## 1. Idle time costs money, if it could earn #business

> Remember that time is money. —— Franklin, 1748

👩 Idling for half a day doesn't just cost the sixpence you spend — it costs the five shillings you could have earned. That's opportunity cost.

> [!claude]
> Right. It assumes the half day could actually have been turned into money: true for a tradesman paid by the day, less so on a fixed salary.
> Over the long run, though, that half day could have become a skill, a piece of work or a connection. The cost is just paid later.

## 2. Money makes money: compounding #business

> Money can beget money, and its offspring can beget more. —— Franklin, 1748

👩 This is compound interest, before anyone called it that.

> [!claude]
> Close, and Franklin knew it well: in his 1785 will he left £1,000 each to Boston and Philadelphia to be lent out at interest for 200 years.
> The idea is his to the letter: money left working keeps multiplying.

## 3. Credit is money #business

> Remember that credit is money. —— Franklin, 1748

👩 Letting someone hold your money late is lending to them for free.

## 4. Reading hour: idle evenings are spent too #for-me

👩 Then my evenings scrolling aren't free either. By Franklin's math, they're my five shillings.

> [!claude]
> That follows, and you're not the first to say it. Bennett makes the same case in 1910 and gets concrete: three evenings a week, ninety minutes each. See [[24 hours a day]].
